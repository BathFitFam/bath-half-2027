Strength, once a week

Twenty-five minutes a week makes your legs more resilient and your running more efficient. Bodyweight is enough to start; a dumbbell, kettlebell or loaded backpack lets you progress.

Warm-up

Before the midweek session, about 10 minutes. Before the Sunday long run, simply start slower than easy for the first 10 minutes.

  1. 5–10 min easy jog
  2. Leg swings: 10 forwards and back, then 10 side to side, each leg
  3. Walking lunges: 6 each leg
  4. High knees for 20 m, then heel flicks for 20 m
  5. A-skips for 20 m
  6. 3 strides of 15–20 s, building from easy to brisk, walking back between

Foam rolling and stretching

Ten minutes after each run, or any evening you feel stiff. Firm pressure, but you should still breathe normally. Pause on tight spots for a few breaths, and avoid bruises, bony bits and anything sharp.

Roll

  • Calves60 s each
  • Quads, front of the thigh60 s each
  • Glutes: sit on the roller, ankle over the opposite knee60 s each
  • Outer hip, just below the hip bone, not down the side of the thigh45 s each

Fuelling and drinks

  • Runs under 75 minutes: no need to fuel. Water if you want it.
  • Runs over 75 minutes: practise taking carbohydrate. A gel, chews or a few jelly babies at about 40 minutes, then every 30–40 minutes (roughly 30–60 g of carbohydrate an hour).
  • Use your long runs to find what sits well. Nothing new on race day.
  • Race-morning breakfast 2½–3 hours before the start: familiar and carb-based, such as porridge, toast, a bagel or a banana.
  • Drink to thirst. More isn’t better.

Winter running

January to March means dark, cold and wet. A few habits keep you safe and consistent.

  • Be seen: hi-vis and a light for dark mornings and evenings.
  • Dress for about 5 °C warmer than it is. Feeling a bit chilly for the first 10 minutes is right.
  • Gloves and a hat are the easiest layers to take off and carry.
  • Ice: swap pace for effort, choose gritted routes or a treadmill, and shorten your stride.
  • Change out of wet kit soon after long runs.

Niggles, illness and missed runs

  • Missed the midweek session? Let it go. Don’t squeeze it in the day before Sunday.
  • Missed a Sunday? Do the long run midweek instead of that week’s session, then carry on. Never double up.
  • A cold above the neck and you feel fine? An easy run is OK. Fever, chest symptoms or aching all over? Rest, then come back with a couple of easy runs.
  • Get pain checked by a physio or GP if it’s sharp, makes you limp, gets worse as you run, or is still there two or three days later.
  • Sleep is training too: aim for 7–9 hours where you can.

Race week and race day

Race week

  • Both runs are short: Sunday 7 March, then the midweek sharpener by Wednesday.
  • Skip strength, or do one light round by Tuesday.
  • Eat normally, with a few more carbs the day before.
  • Saturday: lay out your kit, pin your bib, check your start time and wave, and plan your travel. Expect road closures.

Race morning

  • Breakfast 2½–3 hours before the start.
  • Arrive early. The toilet queues are long.
  • Start at your plan’s pace, not the crowd’s.
  • After the finish: keep walking, eat and drink something, then come and find the Fam.
